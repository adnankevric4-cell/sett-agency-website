summary: "BC Law Ltd is a London-based law firm with an active international practice. We built a brand identity and website that matched the weight of that positioning: serious, reliable, and nothing in excess."
challenge: "BC Law Ltd is a London-based law firm specialising in corporate and commercial law, litigation, immigration, and commercial property. The client, Prof. Bulent Cicekli, is an experienced solicitor with an active international practice and a clear position in the market. The brief was to build a brand identity and website that matched the weight of that positioning: serious, reliable, and nothing in excess."
process: "There is a common mistake in legal branding: an over-reliance on generic symbols of justice, dark navy backgrounds, and impersonal courtroom photography. For BC Law we started somewhere different. The palette was built around Burgundy Noir, a deep dark-red tone that carries authority without coldness, with Muted Ochre introduced as an accent that adds warmth and character. The logo is a monogram, a B and C joined into a single symbol that works both as a seal and as a signature. It is no coincidence that one of the key mockups placed it on a brass stamp. That material and that form communicate institutional weight before anyone reads the firm's name."
solution: "Typography was doing the same work. Richmond Text as the primary face, a classic serif with high legibility and an authoritative character, carries all primary content. Montserrat handles navigation and short informational blocks. The contrast between serif and sans-serif is not purely aesthetic. It signals where the content requires attention and where it needs to be fast and functional."
results: "The website had one concrete problem to solve: how to present a firm with five distinct legal practices without it reading like a service catalogue. The solution was architectural. Each practice has its own page with a clear hierarchy, but the homepage treats them as one coherent offer under a single positioning line. That line gives the firm both a geographic and strategic context that speaks equally to domestic and international clients, which is directly relevant for a solicitor with an active international arbitration practice."
